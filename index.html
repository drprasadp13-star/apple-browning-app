      How to take the photo
      1. Put the pieces in a clear glass dish on a sheet of plain white paper. Leave white paper visible around the dish.
      2. Shoot straight down, with the dish filling about a third of the frame. Use even light from above; avoid side light from a window or desk lamp, flash and strong reflections.
      3. Set the number of pieces in the photo before you shoot.
      4. For the most direct measure, use the comparison mode with a photo of the same dish taken just after cutting, on the same phone.
      What the numbers mean

      The app finds the cut pieces, balances colour against the nearby white paper, and converts the pieces to CIELAB. In single-photo mode a ridge model, trained on photos of one apple taken with three Android phones over 0 to 120 min, estimates the colour difference (ΔE*ab) from the freshly cut state. In comparison mode the app measures ΔE*ab directly between your two photos.

      Levels follow fixed colour-difference bands: 0 below 1.5, 1 from 1.5 to 3, 2 from 3 to 6, 3 at 6 or more. They are image-derived levels, not colorimeter readings.

      On a held-out phone the single-photo estimate was off by about ΔE units on average. The model was trained on a single apple in one session, so treat results as a research prototype.
